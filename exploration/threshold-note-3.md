   candidate, and for the Pfitzinger base that rule is the book's.** The other

   card.** The four candidates are four **sources**, not four rules. The HR
   estimate sits beside the watch's own number, which is the same kind of
   thing. Keep four.

   The load model keeps the watch's zone model as its reference (the zone-5
   floor), and the load history does not change because an athlete chose a
   different number. The load model's own inputs are examined in **stage 2**
   of the zone-model migration, as a separate change with its own
   announcement. So the athlete's threshold is used for the **plan's pacing**
   and the **80/20 band**, and nowhere else.
5. **One threshold at a time — dated, and tied to the base.** The choice
   carries the base it was made for. If the athlete adopts a different base
   later, that base may come with a new choice, and the old one stays in the
   dated history. **And: the plan's pacing names the threshold it was paced
   with** — the value and the date — because a plan adopted in March and
   still running in July is paced by March's number, and the card says so.
6. **Yes — a typed candidate may carry a pair** (a pace band and/or a heart
   rate). The book's LT is a pace band and the watch's is a heart rate, so
   the typed candidate must be able to be either or both.
