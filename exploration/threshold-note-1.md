Parent: one pointer for the threshold design — no extra work for you, but it
changes what the field test's card says.

The pace engine is adding a **physiological-cost curve** (%maxVO₂ against
time, from the athlete's VDOT). On that curve:

* the athlete's **own races** sit as dots;
* the **30-minute field test** sits as a marker at 30 minutes.

So the threshold card's field-test candidate can **point at the curve**
rather than restate what the curve shows — the estimate's meaning ("a
30-minute effort costs this much") belongs to the curve, and the card's job
is the estimate itself: the rule named, the number, the date, its limits.

Two things to hold onto:

1. **Do not draw the method's HR bands on that curve.** %maxHR and %maxVO₂
   are not convertible without the athlete's lactate relation, which Stride
   does not have. If your design shows a band against the field test's
   estimate, it must be on the **heart-rate axis of the athlete's own
   history** — not on the VO₂ curve.
2. The field test's estimate stays **an estimate from a named rule**, with the
   rule's formula and its limits in the sentence — the same register as the
   race-derived LT pace candidate, and equally clearly *not* a verdict.

If your prototype has a natural place to show the estimate against the
athlete's **own** heart rates (the runs they have done at those durations),
that is welcome — it is the athlete's data, and it is exactly the kind of
side-by-side the product does well. But it is not the curve.
