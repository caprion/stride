Your heart rates — one small fact, declared once

Your maximum heart rate, your resting heart rate, or both — each with where it came from and when it was measured. The app works from what you enter: it derives your zones, uses them wherever a band is needed, and never asks again.

The prototype is synthetic. Every number is read from exploration/athlete.js — the canonical athlete; the markup holds no number of its own. The watch's zone model is the athlete's dated fact of 3 Nov 2026; the declared pair is new synthetic data added to that file for this prototype. The rule, the citation and the trade-off are in exploration/heart-rate-setup.md.

1The rig's page — a fourth choice, beside the other three

The rig's page lists the things only the athlete can give, side by side, unranked, with nothing graded and nothing asked for. The heart rates join the race, the threshold and the programme as a fourth card. The heart rates are typed in the app, under Settings › Training — the page says where, the way the threshold's card says where the candidates are.

2The app's card — Settings › Training › Your heart rates

Two fields. Each carries a value, where it came from (a closed list of five, so nothing is free text the app has to read) and when it was measured (which may be left empty). The day the declaration was entered is always shown. Either field may be left empty.

3The two sets of zones, on one axis

The bands from the heart rates you entered, and your watch's own zones, drawn on the same scale so the difference is a picture before it is a sentence. The amber ticks are your watch's floors; the filled bands are the ones the app works from. The numbers under the axis are the differences at the five boundaries.

4The five states

Nothing entered; only a maximum; only a resting rate; both; and changed later. A missing value is a missing value with a reason — never a zero and never a default.

5The two facts, side by side

The watch's zones are the watch's own configuration, dated by the day it was read. The bands from the heart rates you entered are the athlete's own, dated by the day they were entered. Two facts. The app states both, and states that they differ — as a fact, not a correction.

6What the app does with it, without being asked

1It derives the bands
Garmin's published five-zone ladder — 50 %, 60 %, 70 %, 80 %, 90 % — applied to the range the declaration supports: the heart-rate reserve (maximum minus resting) when both numbers are there, the share of the maximum when only the maximum is. The bands are recomputed on read; nothing derived is stored.
2It uses them wherever a band is needed
The ledger's intensity rows, the run detail's own band minutes, the boundaries wherever they are drawn. Every answer names the basis it used and the day it was entered.
3It re-derives them when the pair changes
The earlier declaration stays in the history with its own date, its own sources and the bands it gave. Nothing is overwritten.
4It states the difference
Where the watch's zones exist, the app draws both and says they are different. It does not say the watch is mis-set, and it does not say the declared numbers supersede them.
5It never asks again
The entry is not re-prompted, nothing nags, and no screen says the numbers are old. The load model, the plan's own bands, the threshold and the watch's own zone minutes are untouched.

No verdict. Nothing here says the watch is wrong, that the declared numbers are better, or that either one wants changing.

No blending. No answer mixes a band from one fact with a band from the other, and no answer prefers one silently.

No defaults. A missing heart rate is a missing value with a reason. Nothing is 0 bpm.
