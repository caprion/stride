The method lens — the picture, the caption, and the words

A prototype of the opt-in method lens. The athlete chooses a method; the app draws the athlete’s own numbers with the method’s published number marked on the scale, draws the absence as a gap in a twelve-week strip, and then names the counts and the date in one caption. The rule is quoted and attributed in its own block, the words underneath carry the numbers with their period and their reason, and nothing anywhere draws a conclusion.

The data is synthetic — the same athlete as exploration/stimulus-ledger.html: 25 weeks, 135 runs, and a window of 2 September – 24 November 2026 with 61 runs and 52 h 46 m of moving time. The zone model is HR_MAX, maximum heart rate 183 bpm, read on 1 September 2026 and in force for the window; the athlete’s cap is 139 bpm, entered on 12 June 2026. The rules, their sources and the sentences are in exploration/method-lens.md.

1The control is the athlete’s

A list of radio-style options, off by default, unranked, with no recommendation anywhere. The three methods whose rules a watch cannot measure are listed with their reason and are not selectable — a refused method does not vanish silently. Selecting an option changes the card below; the prototype is live on this page.

2The three lenses, drawn

One picture per card, and the same picture each time: the window’s moving time by heart rate, with the method’s own number marked on the scale — the athlete’s time in azure, the method’s number in neutral ink, and nothing on the scale coloured or labelled as a judgement. Under it, the twelve-week strip: a filled cell for each week in which the heart rate reached the method’s number, an empty cell for each week in which it did not. The hole is the absence; the caption names the date.

6What the card does not say

No verdict. Nothing in the card says the training was unbalanced, that the athlete needs threshold work, or that a stretch of weeks with no time above a ceiling is a problem. The card says when the last one was and how the time moved; the reader draws the conclusion.

No colour that means anything. The athlete’s own time is azure; the method’s number is a neutral mark. No side of the scale means anything, and no label says which side the athlete is on — “above” and “below” are the method’s own arithmetic, and they appear only where the method’s number is named.

No target drawn on the athlete’s data. A rule that states a target — 80 % of the time at low intensity — cannot share a scale with the athlete’s share, because the picture would be the verdict. That is a second reason the 80/20 rule is not a lens.

No link between the rule and the history. The two blocks are two facts, side by side: no arrow, no “so”, no percentage of a target reached.

No score, no grade, no traffic light. There is no number that says how well the athlete did against the rule, and the rule’s own target is never turned into one.

No approximation. A rule stated in blood lactate or in marathon pace is stated, and no numbers are given under it. The app offers nothing in place of a measurement it cannot make — and draws nothing either.

No advice. No suggestion, no “next week”, no plan.

No miss. The absence is a date — and the hole in the strip is drawn before it is named. The word “missed” is on the product’s ban list, and the lens is the reason it can be.

No zero where a value is missing. A window whose runs carry no heart-rate samples has no value and says why, and draws nothing. A measured zero is a measurement and is shown as one.

No default. The lens is off until the athlete turns it on, and nothing on the screen says which one to choose. A refused method is listed with its reason and cannot be chosen.
