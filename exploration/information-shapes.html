Why C and not D

  • The sentence leads, so the reading is the first thing read. On a phone the athlete reads the top of the screen; D makes them read a chart before they get to the point.
  • One idea, one picture, one sentence — the sentence is the reading of that picture, never a paragraph bolted under a chart about something else.
  • Each picture earns its place because it carries one idea that a sentence alone would flatten.

How many pictures?

  • One per idea, and no more. Three on the phone. The radar is the fourth, and it is the summary rather than an idea.
  • The charts stay the charts. The picture is the evidence; the sentence is the reading. Neither replaces the other.
  • Nothing is a chart for the sake of it. A streak is a number and reads as a number. A load is a shape and reads as a shape.

4What the mock does not settle

Still open

  • How many ideas on the phone? Three is the mock. Four is the ceiling before the reading stops being a reading.
  • Does the reading ever mention the plan? Comparing training against the athlete's own plan is factual — and the easiest place to slide into "you fell short". The setup's challenges are the safer version of the same idea.
  • Does the reading change when the range changes? It must, or it is a caption on a chart rather than a reading of a window.
  • Who writes it? The core supplying the facts and a model connecting them — the hybrid — needs a register in the voice contract, and a cache keyed on the window.

Settled enough to build

  • One idea, one picture, one sentence. The sentence is the reading of that picture, and never a paragraph under a chart about something else.
  • The reading leads; the picture is the evidence. Sentence-led, on both surfaces.
  • Encouragement comes from the pattern — "twelve weeks, every week" — never from an adjective about the athlete and never from an instruction.
  • The yardstick is you. Every comparison is against the athlete's own past.
  • The index becomes a way in: rows of links, not rows of figures.
