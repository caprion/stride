The cards as pictures

The wall after D11, redrawn. Eleven cards, each read as a picture first: the numbers are labels on the picture, absence is white space, and the caption says only what the picture cannot — the period, and the reason something is missing. Azure and amber, no red, no score, no grade. Synthetic data: one athlete, 7 Sep – 24 Nov 2026, 58 runs. Phone and desktop widths.

The test each card is held to

  • No card is a list of sentences. A card that cannot be drawn is merged or dropped.
  • No caption repeats a number the picture shows. A number the picture can carry is a label on the picture.
  • Absence is drawn. An empty cell, an empty column, a gap — white space, not a clause.
  • The caption names the period, and the reason a row is empty. Nothing else.
  • Every string is checked against the ban list — should · must · take the day · rest day · go easy · missed · bad · poor · fail · behind · good · red · readiness score · not recovered · you are ready

·The eleven cards

Six on Progress, five on the athlete profile. Each is shown in its ordinary state on the phone and on the desktop, then its states — the empty window, the one run, the missing model, the missing race time, the plan not adopted. A state is a fact, not a placeholder.

1This weekProgress

The picture: seven columns, one per day, each holding the session drawn to its length and its kind; the plan's own pacing shown over it as a faint outline. The previous week's same days sit in grey beside them, so the comparison is in the picture. The caption: the period. The states: no run recorded this week (seven empty columns); no plan adopted (no outlines).

2Where the time wentProgress

The picture: the weeks of the window as columns, the bands as rows, each cell filled in proportion to the time that band held that week — a density, not a category. A marker on the last filled cell of each row, dated. The row label carries the numbers, compactly. Below it, the two shape rows: the window's long runs as a bar series with the largest held out, and the repeated work as marks on the strip of weeks where the watch recorded a workout step. The caption: the period and the basis. The states: one run in the window; no zone model (the band rows have no value, the shape rows still answer); no workout step recorded (the repeated-work row says so in one short line).

3The loadProgress

The picture: the fitness and fatigue curves over the window, the form filled as the area between them, today's value a dot. The caption: the model's own constants and the scenario. The states: no run recorded in the window; no zone model (the load needs the model's maximum heart rate).

4The weeksProgress

The picture: the weekly bars, the typical week as a line across them, and under each bar the days that held a run as marks. The caption: the period. The states: no run recorded in the window (twelve empty columns).

5The paceProgress

The picture: the athlete's own scatter — pace against heart rate, one point per run — with the training paces as the ladder's marks on the pace axis. The caption: the period, and the race the paces came from. The states: no race time entered, so the ladder has no value and only the scatter is drawn.

6Bests & milestonesProgress

The picture: a timeline — distance against date — with every run as a dot and the records marked. The caption: the period. The states: no run recorded in the window (an empty axis).

7Your shapeProfile

The picture: the seven facets as seven horizontal gauges on a common scale, each labelled with its value, each with the way it moved against the previous window. The caption: the period and the comparison. The states: no run recorded in the window (no gauges).

8The monthsProfile

The picture: twelve columns of monthly volume, each column split by the zone mix. The caption: the period. The states: no zone model (the columns carry distance, the mix has no value).

9The scheduleProfile

The picture: a week × hour grid — seven rows, twenty-four columns — with each run a mark sized by its distance. That is the shape of the athlete's week; three tables of numbers was not a picture. The caption: the period. The states: no run recorded in the window (an empty grid).

10The habitProfile

The picture: the streak as a row of day marks, and the Eddington as a ladder of ticks — one tick per distance, each as tall as the number of days that reached it. The caption: the period. The states: no run recorded in the window; too few days for the Eddington to have a value.

11The goalsProfile

The picture: the challenge weeks as a small grid of met and not-met marks, and the plan's own sessions for the week as marks under it — done, planned, planned and the day gone by. Never red; a mark that is not met is an empty square. The caption: the period and the plan. The states: no plan adopted (the session row is absent, and says why).
