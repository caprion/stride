The athlete profile, and the setup that goes with it

A proposal to argue with, not a decision. The question: should the long-term picture and the setup become a page of their own? The short answer is yes, and the reason is not "the long term is a different horizon" — it is that a coach's voice needs a long horizon to be honest. At twelve weeks you cannot say "you are more consistent than you were"; at twelve months you can, and it is a fact.

So: Progress answers "how is my training going?" — the work: the load, the volume, the comparisons, the intensity, the pace, the plan. The athlete profile answers "what does my training look like?" — the pattern: the shape, the habits, the schedule, the run lengths, the seasons. And the setup lives inside the profile, because the profile is the thing you set up: what you want to know, what you want to be consistent about, how much you want to read.

3What the split buys, and what it risks

What it buys

  • The horizon bug goes away by construction. The radar today compares 4 and 13 weeks whatever the range selector says. On its own page, the scope is on the page and every card in it uses it.
  • The coaching voice gets a place where it is honest. "You are more consistent than you were" is a fact over ninety days and a guess over twelve weeks.
  • The setup stops being an orphan. Right now it has nowhere to live; the profile is the thing you set up.
  • Two radars become one. /progress/shape and /progress/profile draw the same six axes twice.
  • Progress gets a spine. Today → Progress → the athlete profile: the next session, then the training, then the pattern.

What it risks

  • The profile can drift into a scoreboard. "Your fitness is 72" is what this page is one bad decision away from. The guard: every number names its period, and there is no composite.
  • Five destinations. The nav goes from four to five. That is the cost of the split, and it has to be earned.
  • Some cards fit both sides. The pace is about a run and about a trend; the zones are about the training and about the pattern. The cut has to be argued case by case, not by horizon alone.
  • Two places for the year. The year has to end up in one place, or the seasons are read twice.

4What this prototype does not decide

Open

  • Is the profile a destination, or a card that opens one? The mock makes it a destination. A fifth destination is a real cost.
  • Does the reading sit on Progress, on the profile, or on both? The mock puts a short one on each: the training on one, the pattern on the other. That is the "how much do you want to read" setting doing two jobs.
  • Is the setup a page or a sheet? The mock makes it a page, because it is asked once and it is worth reading.

Settled enough to build

  • The scope picker: 30 / 90 / 120 days, on the profile, replacing the fixed 4-and-13-week windows.
  • One radar at one route, and the profile owns it.
  • The habits together — streaks, challenges, Eddington — on the profile.
  • The setup asks three questions and shapes the page rather than a separate feature.
