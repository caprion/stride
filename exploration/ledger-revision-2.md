## 1. The absence row needs its caveat, on the card

**Seiler (2010)**: heart-rate time-in-zone **under-counts hard running and over-counts easy running** (HR lag). So "the last run in Z4–Z5 was 40 days ago" is reporting a property of the watch's heart-rate lag as much as a property of the training.

So:
* the absence sentence must **not** stand alone — the card carries a line, in the product's own voice, saying that heart-rate time in zone counts hard running slowly and easy running generously, and that the absence row is the watch's answer as much as the athlete's. No banned words, no verdict; it is a statement about the measurement.
* the absence is **corroborated by the run's own structure** where the structure is available: the lap structure (see the workout-step question below) is a second, independent witness that no hard session happened, because a lap's structure does not depend on heart-rate lag. If the lap data says "no structured session in five weeks" alongside "no time above Z4", the card can say both as two facts. If the lap data is missing, the card says so rather than implying agreement.

This is the same point as the method lens's rule: **the app states what it measured, and what it could not measure.**

## 2. The kinds stay athlete-relative; a method may only ever be a lens

The research's §2.7 table shows the methods disagree most on the two words runners care about most — "threshold" (Daniels: 88–92% of max HR; Norwegian Singles: LT1 at 78–82%) and "easy" (Bakken: below 70% of max; Pfitzinger's general aerobic: 70–81%; Daniels' easy: 65–79%).

That confirms decision (5) in the first revision: the **kinds are the athlete's own bands** (Z1–Z2, Z3, Z4–Z5) and stay that way; a method's vocabulary is never allowed into a kind. Add one line to §1.3 of the note saying so, with the disagreement table as the reason, and pointing at the method lens as where a method's own rule may be quoted **beside** the athlete's numbers (never merged into them).

## 3. Foster et al. — the plan-versus-reality gap (note it, do not build it)

Runners train **harder than planned on easy days and easier than planned on hard days**. So an absent kind can be either "the athlete did not do it" or "the athlete did it differently than the plan said". With v1 anchored on history only (no plan), the ledger cannot tell those apart — so **say so in the note**: this is a known limit of a history-only ledger, and the note should say which question the plan anchor would answer later.

## 4. Two things to add to the states section

* **A window whose runs carry no heart-rate data** — the intensity block has no value; the shape block (longest run, repeated work) may still answer. Say that the card can show one family and not the other.
* **The "no runs in the window" state** — already in §4.1; confirm it says "no run recorded in the window" rather than "rest" (a day with no recorded run may be a forgotten watch).
