Two bases — the ledger's own zone time, and the watch's own zone minutes

The zone model has no history in the source: one record, no date, no effective-from. So the rule each run is measured with the model in force on the day of the run leaves every imported run with no zone time. The card therefore carries two blocks, each naming its own basis, and the two are never added together — plus the weeks where both exist, where the app can check its own arithmetic against the watch's record.

The prototype is synthetic and every number is read from exploration/athlete.js — the canonical athlete; the markup holds no number of its own. The source's own per-zone seconds are not in that file, so the watch's block stands in for them with the athlete's own band minutes; the appendix of exploration/two-bases.md lists every substitution. The rules and the wording are in that note.

1The card, on the phone

Three elements in one card, in this order. Your zones — the app's own zone time, over the moving time, filled only for the weeks that have a model in force. The watch's own zones — the source's own record, over the elapsed time, filled only for the weeks that do not, in five rows of the watch's own zone numbers. Where both exist — the check: the two numbers for the same runs on the same days, and how far apart they are. The solid fill is the app's own zone time; the hatched fill is the watch's own; the amber marks are the weeks where both exist.

2The card, on the desktop

The wide width puts the two blocks side by side and the check beside them, so the two bases are visibly two things. The two blocks' totals are never combined anywhere on this screen.

3The states the card has to hold

Each block answers or says why it cannot, on its own. A block that cannot answer never borrows the other block's numbers. A measured zero is not a missing value.

4What the card does not say

No verdict. Nothing here says the training was unbalanced, that the athlete needs threshold work, or that eight weeks without the app's own zone time is a problem.

No score, and no grade. The check does not rate the watch, the model, or the agreement between them.

No "which is right". The card states the two numbers and the distance between them. It does not pick, and it does not prefer one silently.

No advice. No instruction, no plan, no suggestion.

Nothing added. The two blocks are over two different spans of the same runs — the moving time and the elapsed time. No row of one block sits in a column of the other, and no total on the card adds them.

No boundaries the app does not hold. The watch's rows are the watch's own zone numbers, and the line under them says the boundaries are not recorded.

No zero where a value is missing — and no empty matrix drawn for symmetry: a block that is off is not there.
